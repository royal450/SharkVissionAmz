How to choose a smartwatch

A smartwatch is not about the longest feature list. It's about what you'll actually use daily — and what fits your wrist, your phone, and your budget. Here's the honest breakdown.

Display

AMOLED vs LCD

AMOLED gives deeper blacks, punchier colours and better always-on display support — it looks noticeably premium. LCD is cheaper and still perfectly readable, but looks flatter and is usually dimmer outdoors. If the watch is mostly for steps and notifications, LCD is fine. For looks and outdoor visibility, AMOLED is worth it.

Battery life

Days, not hours

Most budget smartwatches last 5–10 days on normal use, while heavy health tracking or always-on display cuts that significantly. Check real user feedback for battery drain, not just the marketing claim. If charging daily annoys you, prioritise battery life over features.

Calling

Bluetooth calling

Bluetooth calling lets you take calls on the watch when your phone is nearby. It's handy, but call quality depends on the mic and speaker. If calling is important, look for a watch with a speaker and mic, and read verified reviews about call clarity before buying.

Health tracking

SpO2, heart rate, sleep

Most watches now offer SpO2, heart rate and sleep tracking — but accuracy varies a lot. Use these as trends, not medical data. For fitness, step accuracy and workout modes matter more than the number of sensors on the box. Don't buy a watch just because it lists 100+ sports modes.

Water resistance

IP68 and beyond

IP68 handles sweat and splashes fine for daily use. If you swim, look for a proper swim-proof rating and check whether swim tracking is supported. Avoid taking any budget watch into hot water or the sea — ratings don't always cover that.

Strap & size

Comfort matters

A 45mm+ watch can look oversized on slim wrists. Check the case size and strap width — silicone is best for workouts, metal and leather for daily wear. Make sure replacement straps are easy to find and affordable, because the original strap won't last forever.

Budget

Budget ranges in India

Entry-level watches cover basics like notifications, steps and SpO2. The mid range adds AMOLED, Bluetooth calling and better health sensors. Premium watches focus on build quality, accuracy and app ecosystem. Set a hard budget first, then pick what fits inside it.

Checklist

Before you buy

Confirm phone compatibility, check app quality and update history, verify the native display type and battery claim, read verified reviews for sensor accuracy and strap comfort, and always re-check the live price on Amazon or Flipkart — Indian prices move fast.

Smartwatch buying FAQ

Is Bluetooth calling worth paying extra for?

It's handy if you're often away from your phone or on the move. But call quality varies — check verified user reviews for mic and speaker clarity. If you rarely call from your wrist, skip it and spend that money on better display or battery life.

Are budget smartwatch health sensors accurate?

They're fine for spotting trends (steps, resting heart rate, rough sleep patterns) but not for medical decisions. Don't compare numbers across brands — compare your own numbers over time. If accuracy matters, look for brands with a strong track record and verified user feedback.
